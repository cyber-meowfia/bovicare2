CLINICAL FIELD GUIDE

Udder Health & Mastitis Clinical Guide

Early detection of bovine mastitis prevents irreversible glandular tissue fibrosis and safeguards milk quality. Check quarters twice daily before cluster attachment.

1. Subclinical Mastitis (Silent / Hidden) Normal Appearance
• Udder & Milk: Appears visually normal, no clots, no visible heat or swelling.
• Diagnostic Marker: Elevated Somatic Cell Count (SCC > 200,000 cells/mL).
• Detection Method: California Mastitis Test (CMT) creates a thick gel reaction with detergent reagent.
2. Clinical Mastitis (Mild to Moderate) Physical Signs
• Milk Changes: Watery, serous, or discolored milk with visible curds, flakes, or pus stringing.
• Udder Palpation: Affected quarter is warm, firm to hard, swollen, with cow displaying kick-off sensitivity during pre-milking touch.
• Action: Separate quarter milk into discard bucket. Isolate animal to milk last.
3. Severe / Toxic Acute Mastitis (Medical Emergency) ⚠️ Emergency
• Systemic Vitals: High fever (>39.5°C), cold ears, severe lethargy, complete loss of appetite, sunken eyes from dehydration.
• Udder: Extremely painful, purple/blue discoloration (gangrenous), cold teat.
• Action: Immediate urgent veterinary triage required for systemic fluid therapy and targeted parenteral antimicrobials.
🔬 California Mastitis Test (CMT) Interpretation Matrix
Score 0 (Neg): No thickening. Healthy (SCC < 200k).
Score 1 (Weak +): Slight slime dissolves with swirl.
Score 2 (Distinct +): Immediate gel, pulls to center.
Score 3 (Strong +): Egg-white peak forms. Severe.
